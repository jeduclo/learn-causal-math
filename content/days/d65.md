## Theory

### First-stage strength

In the first stage $$X = \pi^\top\mathbf Z + v$$ with $$l$$ excluded instruments, strength is measured by the **concentration parameter** $$\mu^2 = \pi^\top\mathbf Z^\top\mathbf Z\pi/\sigma_v^2$$, estimated through the **first-stage F statistic** for $$H_0: \pi = 0$$. Roughly $$E[F] \approx 1 + \mu^2/l$$. With one instrument, $$F = t^2$$ for the first-stage coefficient.

### Bias towards OLS

With weak instruments, 2SLS is biased in the direction of OLS. A useful approximation (many instruments, homoskedastic errors):

$$
\frac{E[\hat\beta_{2SLS}] - \beta}{\operatorname{plim}\hat\beta_{OLS} - \beta} \approx \frac{1}{F}.
$$

When the instruments are irrelevant ($$\pi = 0$$), 2SLS is centred on the OLS probability limit: weak IV "finds" the OLS answer. Bound, Jaeger and Baker (1995) reproduced quarter-of-birth results with randomly generated instruments.

### Bad inference

The 2SLS t-statistic is not approximately normal when $$F$$ is small: Wald confidence intervals ($$\hat\beta \pm 1.96\,\operatorname{SE}$$) cover less than 95%, sometimes far less when endogeneity is strong. The rule of thumb $$F > 10$$ (Staiger and Stock; Stock and Yogo) was designed to keep bias below about 10% of OLS bias. For a just-identified model, Lee, McCrary, Moreira and Porter (2022) show the usual t-test has correct 5% size only if $$F > 104.7$$; for smaller $$F$$ the critical value must be raised (their tF procedure).

### The Anderson–Rubin test

To test $$H_0: \beta = \beta_0$$, regress $$Y - \beta_0X$$ on the instruments (and controls) and test that all instrument coefficients are zero. Under $$H_0$$, $$Y - \beta_0X = \varepsilon$$, which is unrelated to $$\mathbf Z$$, so the test has correct size **whatever the first-stage strength**. Collecting all $$\beta_0$$ not rejected gives an AR **confidence set**, which can be:

- a bounded interval (strong enough first stage);
- the union of two rays, or the whole real line (weak first stage): the data are honestly uninformative.

### Practice

Report the first stage and its F (robust or effective F with heteroskedastic or clustered errors), the reduced form (an unbiased intention-to-treat estimate whatever the first stage), and AR or tF confidence sets when $$F$$ is not large.

### Exercise 3 · An Anderson–Rubin test

Reduced form 0.06, first stage 0.4. For each $$\beta_0$$, the coefficient on $$Z$$ when regressing $$Y - \beta_0X$$ on $$Z$$ is $$0.06 - 0.4\beta_0$$; take its standard error as 0.02 near the estimate. Is $$\beta_0 = 0$$ rejected? Find the AR 95% interval.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$\beta_0 = 0$$: coefficient 0.06, t = 3: rejected.

**Step 2.** Not rejected when $$|0.06 - 0.4\beta_0| \le 1.96 \times 0.02 = 0.0392$$.

**Step 3.** $$0.0208 \le 0.4\beta_0 \le 0.0992$$, so $$\beta_0 \in [0.052, 0.248]$$.

**Answer:** reject zero; AR interval [0.052, 0.248] around the IV estimate 0.15.

</details>

## Part 2 · Proofs

### Exercise 6 · AR has correct size

Assume $$Y = \beta X + \varepsilon$$ with $$\varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$$ independent of $$Z$$ (fixed). Prove that under $$H_0: \beta = \beta_0$$ the AR statistic has an exact F distribution whatever $$\pi$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Under $$H_0$$, $$Y - \beta_0X = \varepsilon$$.

**Step 2.** Regressing $$\varepsilon$$ on $$Z$$ and testing the coefficients is a classical F test with normal errors independent of the regressors.

**Step 3.** Its null distribution is $$F_{l, n - l - 1}$$, which does not involve $$X$$ or $$\pi$$. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 7 · Irrelevant instruments centre on OLS

Scalar case, no constant: $$X = v$$ ($$\pi = 0$$), $$Y = \beta X + \varepsilon$$, with $$(\varepsilon, v)$$ jointly normal, $$\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon, v) = \sigma_{\varepsilon v}$$, and $$Z$$ independent of both. Show that $$\hat\beta_{IV} - \beta = \frac{\mathbf z^\top\boldsymbol\varepsilon}{\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v}$$ and that, conditional on $$\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v$$, its mean is $$\sigma_{\varepsilon v}/\sigma_v^2$$, the OLS bias.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$\hat\beta_{IV} = \mathbf z^\top\mathbf y/\mathbf z^\top\mathbf x = \beta + \mathbf z^\top\boldsymbol\varepsilon/\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v$$.

**Step 2.** Decompose $$\varepsilon = (\sigma_{\varepsilon v}/\sigma_v^2)v + w$$, with $$w$$ independent of $$v$$ and $$Z$$.

**Step 3.** $$\mathbf z^\top\boldsymbol\varepsilon/\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v = \sigma_{\varepsilon v}/\sigma_v^2 + \mathbf z^\top\mathbf w/\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v$$.

**Step 4.** Given $$\mathbf z^\top\mathbf v$$ (and $$\mathbf z$$), $$\mathbf z^\top\mathbf w$$ has mean 0. $$\blacksquare$$ So the estimator is centred at $$\beta + \sigma_{\varepsilon v}/\sigma_v^2 = \operatorname{plim}\hat\beta_{OLS}$$, with heavy tails from the random denominator.

</details>

### Exercise 8 · When AR sets are unbounded

Just-identified case: let the reduced form be $$\hat\rho$$, the first stage $$\hat\pi$$, with variances $$s_\rho^2, s_\pi^2$$ and covariance 0. The AR set is $$\{\beta: (\hat\rho - \beta\hat\pi)^2 \le c\,(s_\rho^2 + \beta^2 s_\pi^2)\}$$ with $$c = 1.96^2$$. Show that it is unbounded when $$\hat\pi^2 < c\,s_\pi^2$$, i.e. when the first stage is not significant at 5%.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Rearrange: $$(\hat\pi^2 - c\,s_\pi^2)\beta^2 - 2\hat\rho\hat\pi\beta + (\hat\rho^2 - c\,s_\rho^2) \le 0$$.

**Step 2.** If the leading coefficient $$\hat\pi^2 - c\,s_\pi^2 < 0$$, the quadratic tends to $$-\infty$$ as $$|\beta| \to \infty$$, so all large $$|\beta|$$ satisfy the inequality.

**Step 3.** The set therefore contains both tails: unbounded (two rays, or the whole line). $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 9 · F equals t squared

With one instrument, prove that the first-stage F statistic equals the square of the first-stage t statistic.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** The F test of one restriction is $$F = (\text{restricted SSR} - \text{unrestricted SSR})/(\text{unrestricted SSR}/(n - k))$$.

**Step 2.** For a single coefficient, the numerator equals $$\hat\pi^2\sum\tilde z_i^2$$ (FWL), and $$\hat\sigma_v^2 = \text{SSR}/(n - k)$$.

**Step 3.** So $$F = \hat\pi^2\sum\tilde z_i^2/\hat\sigma_v^2 = \big(\hat\pi/\operatorname{SE}(\hat\pi)\big)^2 = t^2$$. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 10 · The reduced form is always informative

Explain why the reduced form $$\hat\rho$$ is a valid estimate of a causal effect (of the instrument) whatever the first-stage strength, and how its sign relates to that of $$\beta$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$Z$$ is as good as random, so the regression of $$Y$$ on $$Z$$ estimates the causal effect of $$Z$$ (an ITT), with standard inference.

**Step 2.** Under exclusion and a constant effect, $$\rho = \beta\pi$$; if the sign of $$\pi$$ is known, the sign of $$\rho$$ is the sign of $$\beta$$.

**Answer:** testing $$\rho = 0$$ is a weak-instrument-robust test of $$\beta = 0$$ (it is the AR test at $$\beta_0 = 0$$). $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 11 · Quarter of birth with many instruments

Using 180 quarter-of-birth × state × year interactions as instruments, a study of returns to schooling gets a first-stage F of 2 and a 2SLS estimate of 0.061; OLS is 0.063. With 3 quarter dummies, F = 30 and 2SLS is 0.081. Which estimate is more credible?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** With F = 2, the relative bias towards OLS is about 50%: the 180-instrument estimate is pulled almost to OLS.

**Step 2.** The 3-instrument estimate has a stronger first stage, and less bias.

**Answer:** the 3-instrument estimate (0.081); many weak instruments manufacture an estimate close to OLS.

</details>

### Exercise 12 · Coverage in a simulation

In the Monte Carlo below (one instrument, strong endogeneity, true $$\beta = 1$$), what happens to the median 2SLS estimate and to the coverage of the Wald and AR intervals as the first-stage F rises from about 1 to about 46?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** F ≈ 1: the median 2SLS estimate is near the OLS limit (1.8); Wald coverage about 88%; AR 95%.

**Step 2.** F ≈ 11: median close to 1, but Wald coverage still only about 93%.

**Step 3.** F ≈ 46: Wald coverage about 95%; AR stays at 95% throughout.

**Answer:** F > 10 removes most bias but not all over-rejection; AR is valid everywhere.

</details>

### Exercise 13 · Distance to a specialty hospital

Distance to a cardiac-care hospital instruments for receiving catheterisation (an invasive heart procedure) in a study of survival. The first-stage t is 1.5. What will the AR confidence set look like, and what can still be reported?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 2.** By Exercise 8, the AR set is unbounded.

### Exercise 14 · Judge leniency designs

Defendants are randomly assigned to judges who differ in leniency; leniency instruments for incarceration. With 200 judges, the first stage uses 200 dummies. What weak-instrument problem arises, and what is the usual fix?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Many instruments, each estimated from few cases: 2SLS is biased towards OLS (many-instrument bias).

**Step 2.** Fix: construct a single leave-one-out leniency measure for each judge (the average incarceration rate of the judge's other cases) and use it as one instrument.

**Answer:** one strong instrument (high F) instead of 200 weak ones; the leave-one-out construction avoids the mechanical correlation with the defendant's own outcome.

</details>

## Verify in Python

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(65); n = 500
print(" pi    mean F   median 2SLS   Wald coverage   AR coverage")
for pi in (0.0, 0.07, 0.14, 0.30):
    F, est, cw, ca = [], [], 0, 0
    for _ in range(2000):
        z = rng.normal(size=n); e = rng.normal(size=n); v = .8 * e + .6 * rng.normal(size=n)
        x = pi * z + v; y = 1.0 * x + e                       # true beta = 1, OLS limit 1.8
        z, x, y = z - z.mean(), x - x.mean(), y - y.mean()
        g = z @ x / (z @ z); r = x - g * z
        F.append(g**2 * (z @ z) / (r @ r / (n - 2)))
        b = z @ y / (z @ x); u = y - b * x
        se = np.sqrt(u @ u / (n - 2) * (z @ z)) / abs(z @ x)
        cw += abs(b - 1) / se < 1.96; est.append(b)
        w = y - 1.0 * x; h = z @ w / (z @ z); rr = w - h * z     # Anderson-Rubin at beta0 = 1
        ca += abs(h / np.sqrt(rr @ rr / (n - 2) / (z @ z))) < 1.96
    print(f"{pi:.2f}   {np.mean(F):6.1f}   {np.median(est):10.3f}   {cw / 2000:12.3f}   {ca / 2000:10.3f}")

# Exercise 3: AR interval
print("AR interval:", np.round([(0.06 - 1.96 * .02) / .4, (0.06 + 1.96 * .02) / .4], 3))
```

## Key takeaways

- Weak instruments bias 2SLS towards OLS (roughly by a factor 1/F) and distort t-based inference.
- Report the first-stage F, the reduced form, and Anderson–Rubin (or tF) confidence sets; F > 10 limits bias but does not make t-tests reliable.
- AR sets are valid for any strength and become unbounded when the first stage is not significant: an honest "we don't know".
