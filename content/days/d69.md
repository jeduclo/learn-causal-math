## Theory

### The model

$$
Y_{it} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \beta D_{it} + \varepsilon_{it},
$$

where $$\alpha_i$$ captures everything constant within unit $$i$$ (ability, location, management quality) and $$\lambda_t$$ everything common to all units in period $$t$$ (business cycle, national policy). Both may be correlated with $$D_{it}$$. This is the **two-way fixed effects (TWFE)** model.

### The within transformation

**One way.** Subtract unit means: $$\tilde Y_{it} = Y_{it} - \bar Y_i$$. Then $$\tilde Y_{it} = \beta\tilde D_{it} + \tilde\varepsilon_{it}$$ (plus time effects), and $$\alpha_i$$ is gone.

**Two way** (balanced panel). Double-demean:

$$
\ddot Y_{it} = Y_{it} - \bar Y_{i\cdot} - \bar Y_{\cdot t} + \bar Y_{\cdot\cdot},
$$

which removes both $$\alpha_i$$ and $$\lambda_t$$. Then

$$
\hat\beta_{TWFE} = \frac{\sum_{i,t}\ddot D_{it}\,Y_{it}}{\sum_{i,t}\ddot D_{it}^2}.
$$

By Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (Day 28), this equals the coefficient on $$D$$ in a regression with a dummy for every unit and every period (LSDV).

### What identifies β

Only **within-unit changes** in $$D$$ (relative to other units' changes in the same period) identify $$\beta$$. Units whose treatment never changes contribute to estimating time effects but not to the comparison of their own treated and untreated periods.

### Assumptions

**Strict exogeneity:** $$E[\varepsilon_{it} \mid D_{i1}, \dots, D_{iT}, \alpha_i] = 0$$: past, present and future treatment are unrelated to the shocks. It fails with **feedback** (a bad health shock today changes insurance tomorrow), and with time-varying confounders. Fixed effects remove only what is constant within units (or common within periods).

### Special cases

- Two periods: FE = first differences: regress $$\Delta Y_i$$ on $$\Delta D_i$$.
- Two groups × two periods with treatment in the last cell: TWFE = the 2×2 DiD (Day 67).

### Inference

Outcomes within a unit are correlated over time. Conventional standard errors treating unit-periods as independent can be far too small (Bertrand, Duflo and Mullainathan, 2004). Cluster standard errors at the unit level (or the level of treatment assignment, such as the state).

### Exercise 2 · Two-way demeaning

A 2 × 2 panel: unit A has $$Y = (1, 3)$$, unit B has $$Y = (2, 6)$$ in periods 1 and 2. Compute $$\ddot Y$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Unit means: A 2, B 4. Period means: 1.5 and 4.5. Grand mean 3.

**Step 2.** A1: $$1 - 2 - 1.5 + 3 = 0.5$$. A2: $$3 - 2 - 4.5 + 3 = -0.5$$.

**Step 3.** B1: $$2 - 4 - 1.5 + 3 = -0.5$$. B2: $$6 - 4 - 4.5 + 3 = 0.5$$.

**Answer:** $$\ddot Y = (0.5, -0.5; -0.5, 0.5)$$: rows and columns sum to zero.

</details>

### Exercise 3 · What fixed effects remove

In a regression of wages on union membership with worker fixed effects, which of these confounders are removed? (a) Innate ability. (b) A promotion that also leads the worker to join the union. (c) Region of birth. (d) Economy-wide wage growth.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** (a) and (c) are constant within worker: removed by $$\alpha_i$$.

**Step 2.** (d) is common to all workers in a year: removed by $$\lambda_t$$ (if time effects are included).

**Step 3.** (b) varies within worker over time: not removed.

</details>

## Part 2 · Proofs

### Exercise 6 · Demeaning removes unit effects

Prove $$\tilde Y_{it} = \beta\tilde D_{it} + (\lambda_t - \bar\lambda) + \tilde\varepsilon_{it}$$, so $$\alpha_i$$ disappears.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Average the model over $$t$$ for unit $$i$$: $$\bar Y_i = \alpha_i + \bar\lambda + \beta\bar D_i + \bar\varepsilon_i$$.

**Step 2.** Subtract from the model: $$\alpha_i$$ cancels. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 7 · Double demeaning

In a balanced panel, prove that $$\ddot Y_{it}$$ removes both $$\alpha_i$$ and $$\lambda_t$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$\alpha_i$$ appears in $$Y_{it}$$ and $$\bar Y_{i\cdot}$$ (as $$\alpha_i$$), and in $$\bar Y_{\cdot t}$$ and $$\bar Y_{\cdot\cdot}$$ (as $$\bar\alpha$$): $$\alpha_i - \alpha_i - \bar\alpha + \bar\alpha = 0$$.

**Step 2.** $$\lambda_t$$ appears in $$Y_{it}$$ and $$\bar Y_{\cdot t}$$ (as $$\lambda_t$$), and in $$\bar Y_{i\cdot}$$ and $$\bar Y_{\cdot\cdot}$$ (as $$\bar\lambda$$): $$\lambda_t - \bar\lambda - \lambda_t + \bar\lambda = 0$$. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 8 · FE equals FD with two periods

With $$T = 2$$, prove that the one-way FE estimator equals the first-difference estimator (no time effects).

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Demeaned values: $$\tilde Y_{i1} = -\Delta Y_i/2$$, $$\tilde Y_{i2} = \Delta Y_i/2$$; similarly for $$D$$.

**Step 2.** $$\sum_t\tilde D_{it}\tilde Y_{it} = \Delta D_i\Delta Y_i/2$$ and $$\sum_t\tilde D_{it}^2 = (\Delta D_i)^2/2$$.

**Step 3.** $$\hat\beta_{FE} = \sum_i\Delta D_i\Delta Y_i/\sum_i(\Delta D_i)^2$$, the FD estimator (through the origin). $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 9 · TWFE in the 2×2 case

Two groups, two periods, $$D_{it} = G_i \times \text{Post}_t$$. Prove $$\hat\beta_{TWFE}$$ is the DiD of means.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** With unit and period dummies plus $$D$$, the model has as many parameters as needed to fit each unit's level, each period's level and the treated cell: it is the saturated DiD regression at the group level (unit dummies nest the group dummy).

**Step 2.** By Day 67, Exercise 7, the coefficient on $$G \times \text{Post}$$ is the DiD of means; averaging units within groups does not change it in a balanced panel. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 10 · Feedback violates strict exogeneity

Suppose $$D_{it} = \rho\,\varepsilon_{i,t-1} + u_{it}$$ (last period's shock affects this period's treatment). Show $$E[\tilde D_{it}\tilde\varepsilon_{it}] \ne 0$$ in general, so FE is biased.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$\tilde\varepsilon_{it} = \varepsilon_{it} - \bar\varepsilon_i$$, and $$\bar\varepsilon_i$$ contains $$\varepsilon_{i,t-1}$$ for every $$t \ge 2$$.

**Step 2.** $$\tilde D_{it}$$ contains $$\rho\varepsilon_{i,t-1}$$ and, through $$\bar D_i$$, all past shocks.

**Step 3.** So $$E[\tilde D_{it}\tilde\varepsilon_{it}]$$ includes terms like $$-\rho\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon)/T$$ that do not cancel.

**Answer:** feedback biases FE (by order $$1/T$$); first differences with lagged instruments (Arellano–Bond) are one remedy. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

## Part 3 · Applied

### Exercise 11 · The union wage premium

A cross-sectional regression of log wages on union membership gives 0.18; with worker fixed effects, 0.08. Interpret both and the identifying variation of the second.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Cross-section: compares union and non-union workers, mixing the union effect with permanent differences (skills, sector).

**Step 2.** FE: compares the same worker's wages in union and non-union years, removing time-invariant differences.

**Step 3.** Identification comes from switchers; it assumes switching is not driven by wage shocks (strict exogeneity) and that switchers' premium represents others'.

**Answer:** about 10 points of the cross-sectional gap reflect selection on permanent characteristics. (Measurement error in union status also attenuates FE estimates.)

</details>

### Exercise 12 · A fixed-effects estimate by hand

Worker A: union 0, 1, 1; wage 10, 13, 14. Worker B: union 0, 0, 1; wage 20, 21, 24. Compute the one-way FE estimate.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** A: $$\tilde D = (-2/3, 1/3, 1/3)$$, $$\tilde Y = (-7/3, 2/3, 5/3)$$. B: $$\tilde D = (-1/3, -1/3, 2/3)$$, $$\tilde Y = (-5/3, -2/3, 7/3)$$.

**Step 2.** $$\sum\tilde D\tilde Y$$: A: $$14/9 + 2/9 + 5/9 = 21/9$$; B: $$5/9 + 2/9 + 14/9 = 21/9$$. Total $$42/9$$.

**Step 3.** $$\sum\tilde D^2$$: A: $$4/9 + 1/9 + 1/9 = 6/9$$; B: $$1/9 + 1/9 + 4/9 = 6/9$$. Total $$12/9$$.

**Step 4.** $$\hat\beta = (42/9)/(12/9) = 3.5$$.

**Answer:** 3.5. Adding year effects changes the answer (the code computes 2.0), because part of each worker's wage growth is common to both.

</details>

### Exercise 13 · Clustering

A state-level policy is studied with 50 states over 20 years. Conventional SE: 0.01; clustered by state: 0.035. The estimate is 0.08. Compare the t-statistics and explain the difference.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Conventional $$t = 8$$; clustered $$t \approx 2.3$$.

**Step 2.** Outcomes within a state are serially correlated, and the policy is constant for years at a time, so the 1,000 observations carry much less independent information.

**Answer:** cluster by state; the effect is still significant, but much less precisely estimated than it seemed.

</details>

### Exercise 14 · Seat-belt laws and traffic deaths

A TWFE regression of state traffic deaths on a seat-belt law indicator, with state and year effects, finds a 6% reduction. Name a confounder that fixed effects do not remove, and suggest a check.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Time-varying state conditions: a recession reduces driving and deaths, and may coincide with law changes; other safety laws (speed limits, drunk-driving laws) adopted at the same time.

**Step 2.** Checks: control for vehicle miles travelled and other laws; event study for pre-trends (Day 68); placebo outcomes (deaths of pedestrians, unaffected by seat belts).

</details>

### Exercise 15 · Health insurance and health with feedback

A panel regresses self-rated health on insurance status with person fixed effects. Why may strict exogeneity fail, and in which direction might the bias go?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** A health shock this year can make people buy insurance next year (feedback from $$\varepsilon_{it}$$ to $$D_{i,t+1}$$).

**Step 2.** Then insured years follow bad shocks; regression to the mean makes health improve after insurance starts, or, if shocks persist, insured years have worse health.

**Answer:** the sign of the bias depends on shock persistence; event-study plots around insurance changes and lagged-instrument methods help.

</details>

## Verify in Python

```python
import numpy as np

def twfe(Y, D, time_effects=True):
    """Fixed-effects coefficient from a balanced N x T panel by (double) demeaning."""
    dm = lambda M: M - M.mean(1, keepdims=True) - (M.mean(0, keepdims=True) - M.mean() if time_effects else 0)
    Dd = dm(D)
    return (Dd * Y).sum() / (Dd ** 2).sum()

D = np.array([[0, 1, 1], [0, 0, 1.]]); Y = np.array([[10, 13, 14], [20, 21, 24.]])
print("Ex 12: one-way FE", round(twfe(Y, D, False), 3), " two-way FE", round(twfe(Y, D, True), 3))
Y2 = np.array([[1, 3], [2, 6.]])
print("Ex 2:", (Y2 - Y2.mean(1, keepdims=True) - Y2.mean(0, keepdims=True) + Y2.mean()).tolist())

# Union premium: ability drives membership; FE removes it. Clustered vs naive SEs.
rng = np.random.default_rng(69); N, T = 2000, 6
A = rng.normal(size=N)
D = (A[:, None] + rng.normal(0, 1.5, (N, T)) > 0.5).astype(float)
e = 0.7 * rng.normal(size=(N, 1)) + 0.5 * rng.normal(size=(N, T))          # persistent shocks
Y = 2 + 0.08 * D + 0.25 * A[:, None] + 0.02 * np.arange(T) + e
X = np.column_stack([np.ones(N * T), D.ravel()])
print(f"pooled OLS {np.linalg.lstsq(X, Y.ravel(), rcond=None)[0][1]:.3f}  TWFE {twfe(Y, D):.3f}  (truth 0.08)")

# SEs for a state-level policy: conventional vs clustered (one-way FE on a policy constant for years)
S, Tn = 50, 20
reps = []
for _ in range(300):
    P = np.zeros((S, Tn)); start = rng.integers(5, 15, S); P[np.arange(Tn)[None, :] >= start[:, None]] = 1
    P[rng.random(S) < .5] = 0
    u = np.zeros((S, Tn)); u[:, 0] = rng.normal(size=S)
    for t in range(1, Tn): u[:, t] = .8 * u[:, t - 1] + rng.normal(size=S)
    reps.append(twfe(u, P))
print(f"true sampling SD of the placebo estimate: {np.std(reps):.3f}")
Pd = P - P.mean(1, keepdims=True) - P.mean(0, keepdims=True) + P.mean(); ud = u - u.mean(1, keepdims=True) - u.mean(0, keepdims=True) + u.mean()
b = (Pd * ud).sum() / (Pd ** 2).sum(); r = ud - b * Pd
conv = np.sqrt((r ** 2).sum() / (S * Tn) / (Pd ** 2).sum())
clus = np.sqrt(sum(((Pd[s] * r[s]).sum()) ** 2 for s in range(S))) / (Pd ** 2).sum()
print(f"one draw: conventional SE {conv:.3f}, clustered SE {clus:.3f}")
```

## Key takeaways

- TWFE removes unit-constant and period-common confounders by double demeaning; identification comes from within-unit changes in treatment.
- With two periods FE = first differences, and in the 2×2 design TWFE = DiD.
- It needs strict exogeneity (no feedback, no time-varying confounders) and cluster-robust standard errors.
