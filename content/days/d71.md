## Theory

### Cohorts and group-time effects

Let $$G_i$$ be the period in which unit $$i$$ first receives treatment (its **cohort**), with $$G_i = \infty$$ for never-treated units. The building block is the **group-time ATT**:

$$
ATT(g, t) = E[Y_t(g) - Y_t(\infty) \mid G = g],
$$

the effect in period $$t$$ for units that adopted at $$g$$.

### Identification (Callaway and Sant'Anna)

Under parallel trends and no anticipation, with never-treated controls,

$$
ATT(g, t) = E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid G = g] - E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid G = \infty].
$$

Each $$ATT(g, t)$$ is a clean 2×2 DiD: cohort $$g$$ versus never-treated units, between the last pre-period $$g - 1$$ and period $$t$$. With **not-yet-treated** controls, replace the second term by the change among units with $$G > t$$ (untreated through period $$t$$). Already-treated units are never used as controls.

Pre-treatment $$ATT(g, t)$$ with $$t < g$$ (computed relative to the preceding period, or to $$g - 1$$) serve as placebo tests.

### Aggregation

Choose weights for the question asked:

- **Event study:** $$\theta(e) = \sum_g w_g\,ATT(g, g + e)$$, with $$w_g$$ the share of cohort $$g$$ among cohorts observed $$e$$ periods after adoption.
- **Overall:** average of all post-treatment $$ATT(g, t)$$ weighted by cohort size (each treated unit-period equally), or average of the event-study $$\theta(e)$$.
- **By cohort or calendar time** as needed.

Weights are non-negative and transparent, unlike TWFE's.

### Sun and Abraham's interaction-weighted estimator

A TWFE event-study regression with relative-time dummies pools cohorts; Sun and Abraham (2021) show each coefficient can be contaminated by effects at **other** relative times. Their fix: interact relative-time dummies with cohort dummies (using never- or last-treated units as controls), estimate cohort-specific effects $$CATT(g, e)$$, then average them with cohort shares. This equals the Callaway–Sant'Anna event study with never-treated controls in the basic case.

### Other robust estimators

The **imputation** estimator (Borusyak, Jaravel and Spiess) fits unit and time effects on untreated observations only, imputes $$Y(0)$$ for treated observations, and averages the differences. Covariates enter through conditional parallel trends, with outcome-regression, weighting or doubly robust versions.

### Practical choices

Never-treated versus not-yet-treated controls (more data versus fewer assumptions about anticipation); balanced event-time samples (so the event study does not change composition); clustered inference across units.

## Part 2 · Proofs

### Exercise 6 · Identifying ATT(g, t)

Assume (i) no anticipation: $$Y_s = Y_s(\infty)$$ for cohort $$g$$ when $$s < g$$; (ii) parallel trends: $$E[Y_t(\infty) - Y_{g-1}(\infty) \mid G = g] = E[Y_t(\infty) - Y_{g-1}(\infty) \mid G = \infty]$$. Prove the identification formula.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** For cohort $$g$$ at $$t \ge g$$: $$Y_t = Y_t(g)$$; at $$g - 1$$: $$Y_{g-1} = Y_{g-1}(\infty)$$ by (i).

**Step 2.** $$E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid G = g] = ATT(g, t) + E[Y_t(\infty) - Y_{g-1}(\infty) \mid G = g]$$.

**Step 3.** For never-treated units $$Y = Y(\infty)$$, so by (ii) the second term equals $$E[Y_t - Y_{g-1} \mid G = \infty]$$.

**Step 4.** Subtract. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 7 · Not-yet-treated controls

Show the formula remains valid with controls $$\{G > t\}$$ if parallel trends holds for them and none of them anticipates treatment before adopting.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Units with $$G > t$$ are untreated in periods $$g - 1$$ and $$t$$; with no anticipation, their observed outcomes are $$Y(\infty)$$ in both periods.

**Step 2.** Parallel trends with them gives the same argument as Exercise 6, Step 3. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 8 · Anticipation contaminates not-yet-treated controls

Suppose cohort 3 changes behaviour in period 2 (one period early), raising its outcome by $$a$$. Show that $$ATT(2, 2)$$ estimated with never-treated and cohort-3 controls (equal weights) is biased by $$-a/2$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Cohort 3's observed change from 1 to 2 is its untreated trend plus $$a$$.

**Step 2.** The average control change is the untreated trend plus $$a/2$$.

**Step 3.** The estimate is $$ATT(2, 2) - a/2$$. $$\blacksquare$$ With never-treated controls only, there is no bias.

</details>

### Exercise 9 · Aggregations are convex combinations

Show that $$\theta(e) = \sum_g w_g ATT(g, g + e)$$ with $$w_g = P(G = g \mid G + e \le T, G \le T)$$ has non-negative weights summing to 1.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Conditional probabilities over the cohorts observed at event time $$e$$ are non-negative.

**Step 2.** They sum to 1 over those cohorts. $$\blacksquare$$ Hence $$\theta(e)$$ lies between the smallest and largest cohort effects at event time $$e$$, unlike TWFE (Day 70, Exercise 9).

</details>

### Exercise 10 · Why TWFE event studies mislead

In a staggered design, the TWFE event-study coefficient for relative time $$e$$ uses already-treated cohorts as part of the comparison for other cohorts. Using Day 70's Exercise 7, explain why a pre-period coefficient can be non-zero even when parallel trends holds.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Relative-time dummies are estimated jointly with unit and time effects; the time effects are partly identified from already-treated cohorts.

**Step 2.** If those cohorts' effects evolve, the time effects absorb part of their dynamics.

**Step 3.** Every relative-time coefficient (including leads) is then measured against these contaminated time effects, so treatment dynamics leak into the leads.

**Answer:** spurious pre-trends (or spurious flat pre-trends) can arise from treatment effect heterogeneity; cohort-specific estimates avoid this. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 15 · Interaction-weighted event study

Explain to a colleague how to run Sun and Abraham's estimator with standard regression software.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Keep never-treated (or last-treated) units as the control cohort.

**Step 2.** Regress the outcome on unit and time fixed effects plus interactions of each cohort with each relative-time dummy (excluding $$e = -1$$).

**Step 3.** Average the cohort-specific coefficients at each $$e$$ using each cohort's share among units observed at $$e$$, and compute standard errors for these averages (delta method or bootstrap, clustered by unit).

</details>

## Verify in Python

```python
import numpy as np

Y = {2: np.array([10, 13, 15, 17.]), 3: np.array([12, 13, 16, 18.]), np.inf: np.array([8, 9, 10, 11.])}
T = 4
att = {}
for g in (2, 3):
    for t in range(g, T + 1):
        att[g, t] = (Y[g][t - 1] - Y[g][g - 2]) - (Y[np.inf][t - 1] - Y[np.inf][g - 2])
print("ATT(g,t):", {k: float(v) for k, v in att.items()})
theta = {e: np.mean([att[g, g + e] for g in (2, 3) if g + e <= T]) for e in range(0, 3)}
print("event study:", {e: float(v) for e, v in theta.items()}, " overall:", float(np.mean(list(att.values()))))
print("placebo cohort 3 (1->2):", (Y[3][1] - Y[3][0]) - (Y[np.inf][1] - Y[np.inf][0]))

# TWFE on the same cohort-level panel
M = np.array([Y[2], Y[3], Y[np.inf]]); D = np.array([[0, 1, 1, 1], [0, 0, 1, 1], [0, 0, 0, 0.]])
dm = lambda A: A - A.mean(1, keepdims=True) - A.mean(0, keepdims=True) + A.mean()
print("TWFE:", round((dm(D) * M).sum() / (dm(D) ** 2).sum(), 3))

# Unit-level simulation: many units per cohort, noise, the same dynamic effects
rng = np.random.default_rng(71); n = 300
cohorts = np.repeat([2, 3, 99], n); t = np.arange(1, T + 1)
eff = lambda e: np.where(e >= 0, 2 + e, 0)
Yu = rng.normal(0, 2, 3 * n)[:, None] + t[None, :] + eff(t[None, :] - cohorts[:, None]) + rng.normal(0, 1, (3 * n, T))
def att_gt(g, tt):
    tr, nv = cohorts == g, cohorts == 99
    return (Yu[tr, tt - 1] - Yu[tr, g - 2]).mean() - (Yu[nv, tt - 1] - Yu[nv, g - 2]).mean()
print("simulated ATT(g,t):", {(g, tt): round(float(att_gt(g, tt)), 2) for g in (2, 3) for tt in range(g, T + 1)})
```

## Key takeaways

- $$ATT(g, t)$$ compares each adoption cohort with never-treated or not-yet-treated units relative to its own pre-period: no forbidden comparisons.
- Aggregate with explicit, non-negative weights into event-study, cohort or overall effects; keep event windows balanced.
- Sun–Abraham's interaction-weighted event study and imputation estimators solve the same contamination problem in regression form.
