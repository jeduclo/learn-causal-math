## Theory

### The trade-off

For a local linear RD estimator with bandwidth $$h$$ and sample size $$n$$,

$$
\text{Bias} \approx Bh^2, \qquad \operatorname{Var} \approx \frac{V}{nh},
$$

where $$B$$ depends on the curvature of $$E[Y \mid X]$$ near the cutoff and $$V$$ on the outcome variance and the density of $$X$$ at $$c$$.

### MSE-optimal bandwidth

Minimising $$\text{MSE}(h) = B^2h^4 + V/(nh)$$ gives

$$
h_{MSE} = \Big(\frac{V}{4B^2n}\Big)^{1/5} \propto n^{-1/5}.
$$

Data-driven selectors (Imbens–Kalyanaraman; Calonico–Cattaneo–Titiunik, CCT) estimate $$B$$ (using a pilot local quadratic fit) and $$V$$, then plug in. Different bandwidths can be used on each side.

### The inference problem

At $$h_{MSE}$$, squared bias equals one quarter of the variance, so

$$
\frac{\text{Bias}}{\operatorname{SE}} = \frac12.
$$

A conventional 95% interval $$\hat\tau \pm 1.96\,\operatorname{SE}$$ then covers only about 92% of the time, and less when bias is underestimated.

### Fixes

- **Undersmoothing:** use a smaller bandwidth than $$h_{MSE}$$ so bias is negligible; the point estimate becomes noisier and the right amount of undersmoothing is unclear.
- **Robust bias correction (Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik, 2014):** estimate the bias with a local quadratic fit (pilot bandwidth $$b$$), subtract it, and compute a standard error that includes the variability of the bias estimate. With $$b = h$$, the bias-corrected estimator coincides with the local quadratic estimator, and RBC inference is local quadratic inference at bandwidth $$h$$.

**Current practice:** report the local linear estimate at $$h_{MSE}$$ with the RBC confidence interval; show sensitivity to halving and doubling the bandwidth. Coverage-error-optimal bandwidths (smaller than $$h_{MSE}$$) are an alternative for inference.

## Part 1 · Warm-up

### Exercise 1 · An optimal bandwidth

With $$B = 2$$, $$V = 4$$ and $$n = 1{,}000$$, compute $$h_{MSE}$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$h = (4/(4 \times 4 \times 1{,}000))^{1/5} = (1/4{,}000)^{1/5}$$.

**Step 2.** $$\ln 4{,}000 \approx 8.29$$; divided by 5: 1.66; $$e^{-1.66} \approx 0.19$$.

**Answer:** about 0.19 units of the running variable.

</details>

### Exercise 2 · More data

If the sample grows 32-fold, how does $$h_{MSE}$$ change?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$h \propto n^{-1/5}$$; $$32^{-1/5} = 1/2$$.

**Answer:** it halves.

</details>

### Exercise 3 · Coverage at the optimum

With bias equal to half the SE, what is the coverage of a nominal 95% interval?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** The t-statistic is $$N(0.5, 1)$$ around the truth.

**Step 2.** Coverage $$= P(|Z + 0.5| < 1.96) = \Phi(1.46) - \Phi(-2.46) \approx 0.928 - 0.007 = 0.921$$.

**Answer:** about 92%.

</details>

### Exercise 4 · Undersmoothing

To make the bias at most 10% of the SE, by what factor must the bandwidth shrink relative to $$h_{MSE}$$?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Bias/SE $$\propto h^2 \cdot h^{1/2} = h^{5/2}$$.

**Step 2.** We need the ratio to fall from 0.5 to 0.1: factor 5, so $$h$$ shrinks by $$5^{2/5} \approx 1.90$$.

**Answer:** use roughly half the MSE-optimal bandwidth; the SE then rises by $$\sqrt{1.9} \approx 1.38$$.

</details>

### Exercise 5 · Ingredients of RBC

List what is estimated in robust bias-corrected inference.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** The local linear estimate $$\hat\tau_h$$ with bandwidth $$h$$.

**Step 2.** The bias $$\hat B$$ from a local quadratic fit with pilot bandwidth $$b$$.

**Step 3.** A standard error for $$\hat\tau_h - \hat B$$ that includes the variance of $$\hat B$$ and its covariance with $$\hat\tau_h$$.

</details>

## Part 2 · Proofs

### Exercise 6 · Deriving the optimal bandwidth

Minimise $$B^2h^4 + V/(nh)$$ over $$h > 0$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Derivative: $$4B^2h^3 - V/(nh^2) = 0$$.

**Step 2.** $$h^5 = V/(4B^2n)$$, so $$h = (V/(4B^2n))^{1/5}$$.

**Step 3.** The second derivative $$12B^2h^2 + 2V/(nh^3) > 0$$: a minimum. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 7 · Bias is half the SE

Show that at $$h_{MSE}$$, $$\text{Bias}^2 = \operatorname{Var}/4$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** From Exercise 6, $$4B^2h^5 = V/n$$, so $$B^2h^4 = V/(4nh)$$.

**Step 2.** The left side is $$\text{Bias}^2$$; the right side is $$\operatorname{Var}/4$$. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 8 · The convergence rate

Show that at $$h_{MSE}$$ the root mean squared error is of order $$n^{-2/5}$$, slower than the parametric $$n^{-1/2}$$.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$\text{MSE} = B^2h^4 + V/(nh)$$ with $$h \propto n^{-1/5}$$.

**Step 2.** $$B^2h^4 \propto n^{-4/5}$$ and $$V/(nh) \propto n^{-1 + 1/5} = n^{-4/5}$$.

**Step 3.** $$\text{RMSE} \propto n^{-2/5}$$. $$\blacksquare$$ RD uses only data near the cutoff, so it learns more slowly than an experiment.

</details>

### Exercise 9 · Why bias correction needs a new SE

Let $$\hat\tau_{bc} = \hat\tau - \hat B$$. Show $$\operatorname{Var}(\hat\tau_{bc}) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat\tau) + \operatorname{Var}(\hat B) - 2\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\tau, \hat B)$$ and explain why using $$\operatorname{SE}(\hat\tau)$$ alone is wrong.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Variance of a difference.

**Step 2.** $$\hat B$$ is estimated from the same (or overlapping) data with a curvature estimate, which is noisy: $$\operatorname{Var}(\hat B)$$ is not negligible in large samples when $$b$$ is of the same order as $$h$$.

**Answer:** ignoring it understates uncertainty; RBC uses the full variance. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

### Exercise 10 · Choosing the bandwidth by eye

Explain why picking the bandwidth that gives the most significant estimate invalidates inference.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Estimates at different bandwidths are noisy, and some will be significant by chance.

**Step 2.** Choosing the most significant inflates the probability of rejecting a true null above 5% (specification searching).

**Answer:** pre-specify a data-driven bandwidth and report sensitivity across bandwidths, not the best one. $$\blacksquare$$

</details>

## Part 3 · Applied

### Exercise 11 · A scholarship RD bandwidth

A pilot local quadratic fit suggests $$B = 0.5$$ (enrolment probability per squared score unit, in standardised score units), the variance constant is $$V = 2$$, and there are $$n = 5{,}000$$ students. Compute $$h_{MSE}$$, and the bandwidth after a 50% undersmoothing.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** $$h = (2/(4 \times 0.25 \times 5{,}000))^{1/5} = (2/5{,}000)^{1/5} = (0.0004)^{0.2}$$.

**Step 2.** $$\ln 0.0004 \approx -7.82$$; $$/5 = -1.56$$; $$e^{-1.56} \approx 0.21$$.

**Answer:** $$h_{MSE} \approx 0.21$$ sd of the score; undersmoothed about 0.10.

</details>

### Exercise 12 · Coverage in a simulation

In the simulation below (true jump 5, curved regression function, 1,000 observations), compare the coverage of conventional local linear intervals with local quadratic (RBC with $$b = h$$) intervals at bandwidths 0.15, 0.3 and 0.5.

### Exercise 14 · Different bandwidths on each side

Mortality data around a very-low-birth-weight cutoff have many more observations above than below the threshold, and curvature differs. Why might separate bandwidths help?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Each side's MSE depends on its own density, variance and curvature.

**Step 2.** Separate bandwidths use more data where it is plentiful and the function is flat, less where it curves.

**Answer:** lower overall MSE for the jump estimate; CCT-style selectors provide side-specific bandwidths.

</details>

### Exercise 15 · Few observations near the cutoff

An RD on a school-entry age cutoff has only 60 children within a month of the cutoff. What alternative to continuity-based inference is appropriate?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Asymptotic local polynomial inference may be unreliable with so few observations.

**Step 2.** Local randomisation: treat children born within a narrow window as if randomly assigned, check covariate balance, and use Fisher randomisation inference (Day 37).

**Answer:** a local-randomisation analysis in a small window, reported alongside the continuity-based estimate.

</details>

## Verify in Python

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

print("Ex 1 h_MSE:", round((4 / (4 * 4 * 1000)) ** .2, 3), " Ex 11:", round((2 / (4 * .25 * 5000)) ** .2, 3),
      " Ex 3 coverage:", round(norm.cdf(1.96 - .5) - norm.cdf(-1.96 - .5), 3), " Ex 4 factor:", round(5 ** .4, 2))

rng = np.random.default_rng(75)
mu0 = lambda x: 50 + 8 * x + 30 * x ** 2 - 20 * x ** 3
def side(x, y, h, p):
    w = np.clip(1 - np.abs(x) / h, 0, None); m = w > 0
    X = np.column_stack([x[m] ** k for k in range(p + 1)]); W = w[m]
    A = np.linalg.inv(X.T @ (W[:, None] * X)); b = A @ (X.T @ (W * y[m])); r = y[m] - X @ b
    V = A @ (X.T @ ((W ** 2 * r ** 2)[:, None] * X)) @ A                    # heteroskedasticity-robust
    return b[0], V[0, 0]
def rd(x, y, h, p):
    r = x >= 0
    a, va = side(x[r], y[r], h, p); b, vb = side(-x[~r], y[~r], h, p)
    return a - b, np.sqrt(va + vb)
print("  h   LL bias  LL sd  LL cover | LQ bias  LQ sd  LQ cover")
for h in (.15, .3, .5):
    out = {1: [], 2: []}; cov = {1: 0, 2: 0}
    for _ in range(1000):
        x = rng.uniform(-1, 1, 1000); y = mu0(x) + 5 * (x >= 0) + rng.normal(0, 3, 1000)
        for p in (1, 2):
            t, s = rd(x, y, h, p); out[p].append(t); cov[p] += abs(t - 5) < 1.96 * s
    print(f"{h:4.2f}  {np.mean(out[1]) - 5:7.2f}  {np.std(out[1]):5.2f}  {cov[1] / 1000:7.3f} | "
          f"{np.mean(out[2]) - 5:7.2f}  {np.std(out[2]):5.2f}  {cov[2] / 1000:7.3f}")
```

## Key takeaways

- The MSE-optimal bandwidth shrinks like n^(−1/5) and leaves a bias equal to half the standard error.
- Conventional intervals at that bandwidth under-cover; robust bias-corrected intervals subtract the estimated bias and account for its variance.
- Pre-specify a data-driven bandwidth, report RBC intervals, and show sensitivity to the bandwidth.
